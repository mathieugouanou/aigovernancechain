# Public-sector AI transparency record

Use this template for public-facing disclosure, internal accountability and, where applicable, statutory database entries. It follows the UK Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (ATRS) V4 structure: a plain-language Tier 1 summary and specialist Tier 2 detail, including accountability, rationale, deployment context, system/model, development and operational data, and risks/mitigations. It also includes the EU AI Act public-deployer fields in Annex VIII, Section C. Complete “not applicable” fields with a short reason rather than silently omitting them.

The template is a transparency aid. It does not replace an EU database registration, legal notice, privacy notice, DPIA, FRIA, consultation, or any other required record. For restricted law-enforcement or migration uses, do not publish information that must be filed in a secure non-public section.

Sources: [UK ATRS mandatory scope policy](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-mandatory-scope-and-exemptions-policy/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-atrs-mandatory-scope-and-exemptions-policy); [UK ATRS V4 guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guidance-for-organisations-using-the-algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-guidance-for-public-sector-bodies); [EU AI Act, consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/2026-07-27/eng); [AI Act Annex VIII, Section C](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/annex-8).

## Tier 1: Summary information for the public

Write this section for people who do not have technical knowledge. Aim for two or three short sentences for each prose answer.

### What is the tool and why does the public body use it?

[Describe the tool and its purpose. State the public service or process it supports. Explain why the body uses it and what alternatives were considered.]

### What does it do, and what does it not do?

[Explain the AI's role in the process. Say whether it produces a suggestion, score, summary, classification, recommendation or decision. Identify decisions it cannot make.]

### Who may be affected?

[Describe people or groups who may be affected, including service users, residents, patients, pupils, applicants, staff or members of the public.]

### How does a person get a human review or challenge a result?

[Give the actual contact route, available languages/formats and the response process. State whether the person can use the service without the AI where this is an option.]

### What data does the tool use?

[Name the main types of information in plain language. Say whether the data are about the person, supplied by the person, retrieved from records or generated by the tool.]

### What are the main safeguards and limits?

[Describe human review, testing, security, accessibility, known limitations and how the tool is paused if it does not work safely.]

### Is this a pilot or a live service?

[State the current phase and dates. Say whether outputs affect real people or real decisions.]

# Worked example: municipal benefits triage assistant

> **FICTIONAL EXAMPLE.** All names, contacts, systems, results and locations below are invented for illustration. The example does not describe an actual municipality or system.

## Metadata and Tier 1 summary

| Field | Fictional example |
|---|---|
| Public record title | Rivermere Benefits Navigator and Triage Assistant |
| Record reference | FIC-BA-2026-01 |
| Organisation | Rivermere Municipality, Benefits Service (fictional) |
| Jurisdiction(s) | EU Member State (fictional); local and national rules must be identified |
| Record owner | Benefits Service AI Governance Lead |
| Publication date | 2026-10-15 |
| Last reviewed / review due | 2026-10-15 / 2027-04-15 |
| Lifecycle phase | Limited beta for general FAQs plus staff-only shadow-mode triage; no triage output changes case order or a benefit decision |
| Public contact | benefits-ai@rivermere.example |
| Statutory database entry | Not submitted. For an EU Annex III high-risk use, confirm provider and public-deployer registration requirements and applicability dates before operational use. |

**What it does.** The chatbot answers routine benefits-service questions using approved public information. A separate staff-only triage function reads benefit-application metadata and suggests a casework queue and documents for a trained officer to check. It does not decide eligibility, grant or refuse a benefit, reduce or recover a payment, or send a decision to an applicant.

**Who may be affected.** People using the public FAQ chatbot and applicants to the fictional municipality's basic-needs support programme, including people with disabilities, limited digital access or limited proficiency in the local language.

**Human review and challenge.** The chatbot identifies itself as automated, answers general service questions only, and gives a human contact route for case-specific help. A benefits officer checks the original application and may ignore or change every triage suggestion. Applicants can use online, telephone or in-person channels to ask a person to review their case. No adverse benefit decision may rely on the assistant's output alone.

**Data and safeguards.** The FAQ chatbot uses approved public service information and does not collect case-specific personal information. The staff-only triage pilot uses application status, dates, document types and a small text excerpt; it does not use biometric data. The fictional supplier is prohibited from using municipal data to train shared models. The pilot is restricted to staff accounts; triage inputs and outputs are logged with access controls. The municipality has not completed a production impact assessment.

## Tier 2 example

### Owner, provider and decision authority

- Senior responsible owner: Director of Benefits Services (fictional).
- Operational owner: Benefits Operations Manager (fictional).
- Supplier: Rivermere Digital Works Ltd. (fictional).
- Model: QueueAssist 2.3, provided by Rivermere Digital Works Ltd. (fictional).
- Legally accountable decision-maker: authorised municipal benefits officer under applicable fictional national and municipal law.
- Decision role: triage suggestion only. No automated eligibility, award, refusal, reduction, recovery or final priority decision.

### Purpose, scope and risk

- Purpose: flag potentially incomplete case files and suggest a queue for staff review.
- Scope: staff-only shadow pilot on one municipal benefits programme; a limited public beta answers general FAQ content but does not collect case-specific information. Triage outputs do not move cases automatically.
- Out of scope: fraud scoring, eligibility assessment, adverse decisions, ranking by protected traits, cross-programme reuse, biometric identification and use outside Rivermere Municipality.
- Provisional classification: **high risk / likely Annex III point 5(a), verify**. It assists in a process concerning eligibility for an essential public assistance benefit, even though the pilot is shadow-only. A qualified legal reviewer must classify the precise intended purpose. Do not assume pilot status removes any applicable legal requirement.
- Pilot acceptance rule: no production use until the municipality documents legal basis, DPIA, FRIA where required, notice, human oversight, pre-deployment tests, supplier evidence and required database registration.

### Technical and data detail

- System: private municipal web application; model output is a proposed queue label and a checklist of missing document types.
- Inputs: application identifier, dates, document-type codes and limited text excerpt from submitted documents. No training on operational cases.
- Development data: synthetic records and a separately approved de-identified sample. The municipality records provenance and limitations.
- Operational data: real case fields are used only in the restricted shadow pilot; human staff can compare the suggestion with the current manual process. Data remains in the municipality's approved environment.
- Versioning: model/configuration changes require documented testing and written approval by the service owner and security lead.

### Impact assessment and testing

- DPIA: draft only; approval required before production.
- FRIA: not yet completed; required before first covered high-risk deployment if the EU AI Act applies.
- Fairness and accessibility review: planned. Tests must examine language and disability-related impacts where lawful, measurable and appropriate.
- Performance: compare suggested queue labels with independent staff review; report false urgency and missed urgent cases separately. Do not use a single average accuracy score as the sole acceptance test.
- Human oversight: staff receive training; each case is reviewed against source documents; staff can override or disregard outputs; the system can be stopped without blocking application processing.
- Public notice: publish a notice before any operational use that tells applicants the tool may help staff organise case review and gives a human contact route.

### Incidents, recourse and review

- A caseworker who sees an unsafe, inaccurate or discriminatory output reports it to the Benefits Operations Manager and the security contact. The system is paused for affected use pending triage.
- Applicants retain ordinary review and appeal routes under the applicable benefit law. The assistant does not shorten statutory response deadlines.
- Pilot review due 2027-04-15 or earlier after a material model, data, service or workflow change.
